Harassment Free Environment Policy

At Elite Global Sourcing Phil. Inc. we take pride in maintaining a professional, productive and comfortable work environment. In order to ensure our environment remains free from unlawful harassment, the Employer does not tolerate any form of harassment which includes: words or actions which could reasonably be construed as discriminatory or inflammatory, or which violate federal, state or local law, including, but not limited to, harassment related to an individual’s sex, race, color, religion, creed, national origin, ancestry, physical or mental condition including genetic characteristics, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, age or any other protected characteristic.

Defining Harassment

Harassment can take many forms and can include, but is not limited to: words, jokes, signs, photographs, pranks, intimidation, physical contact, threats and acts of violence.

Sexual Harassment

Unlawful sexual harassment includes unwelcome sexual advance, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature when submission to conduct is made either explicit or implicit condition of employment; submission or rejection of the conduct is used as a basis for an employment decision affecting the harassed employee; or the harassment has the purpose or effect of unreasonable interfering with an employee’s work performance or creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment.

Examples of sexual harassment, include but are not limited to: unwelcome sexual advances, offering employment benefits in exchange for sexual favors, visual conduct such as leering, making sexual gestures, displaying of sexually suggestive objects or pictures, cartoons or posters, verbal sexual advances or propositions, verbal abuse of a sexual nature, graphic verbal commentaries about an individual’s body, sexually degrading words used to describe an individual, suggestive or obscene letters, notes or invitations, physical conduct such as touching, assault and impeding or blocking movements. If an employee have any questions about what constitutes harassing behavior, consult with any manager in the Employer with whom the employee feel comfortable.

Reporting a complaint

If an employee feels that an employee is harassing an employee, or if employee observe harassment, the employee should notify his/her immediate manager, HR personnel or a member of the executive management. Elite Global Sourcing Phil. Inc. needs your help in order to resolve these issues. It is, therefore, employee’s responsibility to bring any problems to the employer’s attention so that the employer can investigate promptly and take the necessary steps to correct the situation.

In addition, employees who believe that he/she has been sexually harassed may file a complaint with the local office of the Philippine National Police and/or the Department of Social Welfare and Development, which have authority to remedy violations. The address and telephone numbers are in the telephone directory.

Retaliation is not tolerated.

The employee will not be penalized in any way for reporting a harassment problem and the Employer will not tolerate retaliation towards an employee who has filed a complaint.

Consequences

Any employee who is found to have violated this policy will be subject to appropriate corrective action up to and including termination of employment.

Attendance Policy

To prevent the disruption of efficient workflows due to unexcused or unscheduled absences, employees who cannot report for duty and need to take absence are required to adhere to the following procedures.

If the employee is unable to reach his/her immediate supervisor for any reason, he or she may instead contact Human Resources Department directly.

  • Unless other arrangements have been made with the employee’s immediate supervisor, employees are required to inform his or her supervisor on each day during the period of absence if absence persists more than a day.

  • If employee has been absent for two days or more for medical reason(s), employee will be asked by the supervisor to submit a medical certificate from a physician to Human Resources to verify the medical condition. In addition, if employee absence for more than two days for a medical condition, he or she will be required to submit a fit-to-work certificate from a physician to Human resources.

  • If there is a reasonable cause-requiring employee to leave work before his/her usual workday ends, the employee is required to obtain written authorization from the supervisor. Leaving the working post without supervisor’s permission will be considered as will result in Absence without Official Leave (referred to as “AWOL”) and will be served a Notice To Explain and subject to appropriate disciplinary actions.

  • No Call and No Show (referred to as “NCNS”) or Absence without Official Leave (referred to as “AWOL”) and failure to inform in advance to superiors and HR department for one (1) or more day(s) will be considered as unauthorized absence and a Serious Misconduct and breach of the attendance policy. An Employee who violates this policy will be issued a Notice to Explain and/or warning letter and more than three (3) incidents of the same violation may lead to termination of employment.

  • The HR department will serve employee a Return To Work Order (RTWO) on nd

the following second (2) day upon confirmation of unauthorized absence by the Supervisor of the affected team by means of any direct communication, including but not limited SMS, Call, E-Mail, Messenger, etc. Return To Work Order (RTWO) should be considered as received by the said employee when HR department sent such order via all three means of SMS, e-mail and Messenger if applicable.

  • To prevent an excessive burden on fellow employees as well as our clients, employee with three (3) or more consecutive No Call and No Show (NCNS) absences will be considered as a Grave Misconduct and subject to disciplinary action including immediate termination except justifiable urgent circumstances.

  • Failure to comply and accomplish proper authorized leave procedures will be considered AWOL

5.2.2 Tardiness

All employees are expected to report for work exactly at the start of their scheduled shift. There will be no grace period allowed. An employee who reports late for work will automatically be tagged as tardy. Employees who do not report for work on time or miss all or part of a day’s work will eventually place an undue burden on their fellow employees as well as our clients and the customers. In order to avoid the unfair burden on others due to tardiness, all employees must adhere to the following guidelines:

  • If an employee must come to work later than one’s work schedule, he/she must inform the immediate supervisor on site the exact cause of the incident two (2) hours prior to his/her normal shift starts with a full explanation for such request and the supervisor will provide a written report to HR for documentation. If no supervisor is available to report, contact HR department directly.

  • Every month Human Resources will tabulate the number of tardiness an employee has acquired. If he/she has accumulated a total of 100 minutes or above or more, he/she will be given a Notice to Explain and for unexcused reasons may lead to an issuance of warning letter.

  • To avoid the unfair burden on others due to tardiness, if an employee reports to work late, he or she will be docked for time missed. The employee should understand that Traffic or road condition is not an excusable reason.

  • Failure to show immediate and significant improvement after receiving a warning or suspension could lead to additional disciplinary actions up to and including dismissal.

  • In case when natural disasters or causes occur, such as typhoon, hurricane, flood, fire, earthquake, & etc., which are officially declared by the government or municipality and therefore can be verified, management may decide not to apply tardiness rule during such time of the event. Nonetheless, the employee must inform his or her immediate supervisor on site the exact cause of the incident two (2) hours prior to his/her normal shift starts with an explanation for such request. If no supervisor is available to report, contact HR department directly. Mere Traffic or road condition is not an excusable or justifiable reason.

5.2.3 Establishing Work Attendance Record

  • Employees should establish their work attendance record every working day by clock in & clock out using the company’s official system, which is the MS Shift Application, and in recognized secondary systems when the former is unavailable, whenever they enter or leave the premises for and after work.

  • If the employee fails to properly login and /or out for work via the aforementioned company’s official system which is the MS Shift Application due to reasonable circumstances such as connectivity issues, device malfunction, or other technical difficulties, they must immediately report the issue to both their immediate supervisor and the IT department thru email. The request must be approved by immediate supervisor and acknowledged by IT. Only then may an exception be considered. Otherwise, this will be considered as absence or late from duty.


5.2.4 Attendance Fraudulent Act (Login–Logout)

  • The value of Integrity is vital to the business and well-being of the people at work. Any employee caught logging in or logging out someone else’s attendance record or any activity designed to mislead, defraud, and disrupt the Employer and its authorized representatives is subject to disciplinary action which may include immediate termination.

  • Both parties engaged in the deception, the employee who logged in or logged out and the employee who initiated it, would be held accountable.

  • Falsifying attendance by manipulating the MS Shift Application or submitting false reports to IT or supervisors to gain exceptions will likewise be treated as a fraudulent act and is subject to the same disciplinary consequences.

Professional and orderly behavior is a necessity in order to achieve the Employer’s goal in competitive circumstances. It is also in the future interests of the Employer as well as the employees. The Employer expects employees to understand and accept the discipline as a part of their job requirements and as their responsibility with self- consciousness. 7.2 Types of discipline violations:

Any violations against company policies that are subject to disciplinary actions are classified as either offenses concerning productivity and major responsibility, property and security, health and safety, or conduct and decorum. The degree of offenses range from Misconduct, Serious Misconduct, Grave Misconduct.

  • Misconduct;

  • Serious Misconduct (Yellow Flag);

  • Grave Misconduct (Red Flag)

7.3 Disciplinary Measures

7.3.1 Disciplinary Actions and Documentation Flow

The company enforces a structured, progressive accountability point system in accordance with due process and the Labor Code of the Philippines.

Each violation of the employee handbook will follow the due process procedure:

  • Issuance of a Notice to Explain (NTE)

  • Administrative hearing (if necessary)

  • Submission of a written explanation by the employee

  • Issuance of a formal Notice of Disciplinary Action signed by HR and Management

7.3.2 Accountability Points

All violations are assigned Accountability Points based on severity. These points accumulate over a rolling 365-day period starting from the day the day HR has made the Notice of Disciplinary Action. (Introduce Misconduct – Grave Misconduct)

Accountability Point Thresholds:

  • 1-3 Points = Coaching to Verbal Warning

  • 4-7 Points = Final Written Warning to 30-day suspension depending on severity of the violation

    • Note: Suspensions will be in increments of 1-day, 3-day, 6-day, 30-day

  • 8 Points = 30-day suspension to Termination under Habitual Neglect or Misconduct (Article 297(b), Labor Code)

Note: Preventive suspension without pay of up to 30 calendar days may be imposed at 4 Accountability points or higher, subject to the severity of the violation and due process.

8.2 Complaint Procedures:

Every employee has the right to expect a response for any suggestion, complaint or appeal. However, the employee must present those issues to the supervisor in oral form first. If the results are not satisfactory and some suggestion, complaint or appeal is necessary, timely attention is required to facilitate closure of the situation according to the following procedures.

  • First step: The employee should present those issues to the supervisor in oral form

  • Second step: If the employee is not satisfied with the result of Step 1, the employee should present a written complaint to the supervisor (state the time, location and the nature of the concern using 5W1H principle). The supervisor shall respond in a written form with his/her response and basis within three (3) working days;

  • Third step: If the employee is not satisfied with the result of step 2, he/she may submit the affair to the HR manager. HR manager shall arrange a joint conference together with all parties concerned to solve the problem. HR manager and other management personnel shall respond within five (5) working days. However, the HR manager and Employer reserves the right to extend the response in certain cases that may require a longer period than the prescribed time in order to arrive at a sound and reasonable outcome.

  • Fourth step: If the results do not match the employee’s expectations, the employee may submit the issue to the HR committee. The head management team shall take further action or make the decision within ten (10) business days from the time of the. The solution is regarded as the final decision by the Employer.

*Remarks: Any anonymous complaint will not be recognized as part of the standard process of escalation.

CODE OF ETHICS AND BUSINESS CONDUCTS

The employer will conduct its business in strict compliance with all applicable laws and government regulations wherever we do business. We are committed to the highest level of integrity in our dealings with people and organizations and will conduct business operations within the strict compliance and the spirit of the law.

Each employee has a duty and a responsibility to act in the best interest of the Employer. Employees must maintain the highest degree of honesty and integrity. We, as an employee, has an obligation to avoid any involvement where the possibility of some personal or private advantage or gain could produce a conflict between self-interest and the interest of the Employer.

General Principles

All employees must be aware that violation of this Code of Ethics may result in personal civilor criminal liability for individual employees as well as fines, penalties and severe fi nancial consequences for the Employer. Just as important Employer values its good name and reputation. Therefore, every employee must be familiar with his or her legal obligations and conduct him or herself, at all times, as a responsible citizen and employee. Failure to comply with this Code of Ethics and Business Conduct is grounds for disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

Gifts in the Work Environment

As a rule, the giving or receiving of gifts, rebates, gratuities or other favors of a substantial nature, in return for the purchase or lease of our products are strictly prohibited. However, we realize the need to form a rapport with those we do business with and gifts of modest value that are included in standard business practices, such as an occasional meal, sports game or show are usually appropriate. Two standards by which to judge “appropriateness” are 1) would the “gift” be considered reasonable to public scrutiny and 2) would the “gift” create an obligation or the perception of an obligation to the receiver (employee or the customer/vendor). Employee must ask himself or herself “what is the intention of the gift.” Questions about the appropriateness of either giving or receiving gifts should be referred to our Human Resources Departments.

Kickback/Bribery

It is illegal in the Republic of the Philippines to offer or receive a kickback or bribe. These may be defined as money or items of value being exchanged for the purpose of improperly obtaining business, discounts or special favors. No bribe, kickback or other improper payment shall be made or received by or on behalf of employee or Employer in connection with any of its business. Local practices or customs may be followed with regard to tips or gratuities for services rendered so long as the amount and timing of the gratuity is such that it could not be reasonably construed as a bribe.

Mutual Agreement to Arbitrate Claims

Employee should recognize that differences may arise between Employer and employee during or following his/her employment with Employer and that those differences may or may not be relate to employment. Employee should understand and agree that if differences should arise, both employer and employee Mutual Agree to Arbitrate Claims to gain the benefits of a speedy, impartial, dispute- resolution procedure. Except the Philippine Arbitration Law shall govern the interpretation, enforcement and all proceeding, to the extent that the Philippine Arbitration Law either is inapplicable, or held not to require arbitration of a particular claim or claims, Philippine law pertaining to agreements to arbitrate shall apply. For further detailed information, please refer to “Mutual Agreement to Arbitrate Claims”.
